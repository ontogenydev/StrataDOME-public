# Design principles

- **Sovereignty persists through participation.** A domain can contribute to shared understanding without handing over its source material or surrendering the right to interpret it and decide what follows.
- **No central truth owner.** Shared cognition remains distributed and attributable rather than collapsing into one authoritative view.
- **Origin remains visible.** Material claims stay tied to where they came from and how they entered shared state.
- **Disagreement remains legible.** The protocol does not force unresolved information into a clean answer. Refusal and absence remain visible rather than being recoded as certainty.
- **Evidence is not authority.** Evidence can change what the system believes; it cannot by itself authorize action.
- **Release and acceptance are separate.** A source may release an object without obligating a recipient to accept or act on it.
- **Restrictions propagate.** Changing the form of sensitive information does not silently relax the conditions attached to it.
- **Queries are noncommanding.** A Query requests information; it does not task a source or execute a capability.
- **Fields are emergent.** A Field is constituted from evidence and context; it is not pre-created around a named scenario or administrative boundary.
- **The lattice is not a sovereign.** A broader or more connected view does not gain authority merely by being broader.
