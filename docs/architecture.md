# Architecture overview

StrataDOME is a cross-sovereign epistemic protocol for distributed cognition across autonomous domains. Its architecture assumes that participating systems may differ in authority, ontology, incentives, jurisdiction, and technical substrate.

## Sovereign domains

Each participating domain retains control of protected source state, local policy, local ontology, release decisions, and operational consequences. Shared state receives only what has been deliberately released across a bounded interface.

## Signals

Signals are attributable epistemic acts, not raw database rows or universal truth claims. They carry provenance, standing, uncertainty, and source context.

## Fields

Fields are emergent shared epistemic contexts. They are not predefined by geography, institution, hazard, scenario, or administrative level. A Field exists only when the system can justify its constitution from attributable evidence and governance constraints.

## Queries

Queries arise from unresolved epistemic gaps. They are bounded requests for discriminating information, not commands to another institution. Query generation, delivery, receipt, and confirmation remain separate from operational action.

## Lattice

The wider lattice is an emergent topology of relationships among Fields and participating domains. It is not a central sovereign, universal ontology, or global truth store.

## Provenance and restrictions

Material claims and derived objects preserve lineage, currentness, correction standing, and applicable disclosure restrictions. Downstream processing may preserve or narrow a restriction; it cannot silently broaden one.

## Interferometric cognition

StrataDOME is designed to compare partially independent views across domains and Fields without requiring them to collapse into one shared database or one ontology. The aim is to extract useful cross-domain structure from agreement, contradiction, dependence, uncertainty, and divergence while preserving source sovereignty.
