# Architecture overview

StrataDOME is a cross-sovereign epistemic protocol for distributed cognition across autonomous domains. Participants may disagree about meaning. They may also operate under different authorities or run on different technical foundations. Those differences do not have to disappear before knowledge can move between them.

## Sovereign domains

Each participating domain keeps control of its protected source state and its local rules for interpretation and release. It also retains responsibility for what it does with the result. Shared state receives only what the domain deliberately exposes through a bounded interface.

## Signals

Signals are attributable epistemic acts, not raw database rows or universal truth claims. A Signal stays tied to its source. Uncertainty remains visible, and enough context travels with it for another domain to interpret what it received.

## Fields

Fields are emergent shared epistemic contexts. They are not pre-created around a named scenario or administrative boundary. A Field exists only when the system can justify its constitution from attributable evidence under the rules that govern its participants.

## Queries

Queries arise from unresolved epistemic gaps. They are bounded requests for discriminating information, not commands to another institution. A Query can be formed and delivered without giving the sender authority over what the recipient does next.

## Lattice

The wider lattice is an emergent topology of relationships among Fields and participating domains. It does not own the participants or their interpretation of the world, and it does not become a global truth store.

## Provenance and restrictions

Material claims remain tied to their history and to the conditions under which they are still valid. If information is transformed downstream, those conditions may be preserved or narrowed; they cannot silently become broader.

## Interferometric cognition

StrataDOME is designed to compare partially independent views across domains and Fields without forcing them into one shared database or one ontology. Agreement is informative, but disagreement can be just as useful. Dependence between views also matters. Taken together, these relationships can reveal structure that no participant sees alone while the sources remain sovereign.
